Healthcare

Getting medicines to market faster.

In pharma, most of the work worth automating isn't sitting there labelled as an AI problem. Finding it is half the job.

These are processes people have run by hand for decades — assembling the evidence behind a clinical document, checking whether a trial design has precedent, reconciling a development plan across six functions. Nobody had framed them as automatable, largely because until recently they weren't. So a good part of my work has been sitting with a team, watching the manual process, and making the case with a working prototype rather than a slide.

Then the building starts and pharma data does its best to stop you. A clinical programme produces an enormous amount of information — protocols, study reports, registry entries, regulatory submissions, decades of published literature — and almost none of it arrives structured. Two studies will describe the same endpoint three different ways. And because the output ends up in regulated documents, every claim has to trace back to the sentence it came from.

The effect of getting that right is simple enough. Highly trained people stop spending their week on assembly and go back to judgement. A few weeks off one document is small. A few weeks off every document, across a whole programme, is a medicine reaching the people waiting for it sooner.
